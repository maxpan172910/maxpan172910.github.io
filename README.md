I am a Electrical and Computer Engineering PhD student at Georgia Tech. As an undergraduate, I double-majored in physics and computer science at UC Berkeley. In addition, as an undergraduate, I did computational materials research using density functional theory (DFT). My background in physics gave me the ability to tackle difficult concepts while my background in computer science taught me how to turn ideas into working implementations. Afterwards, in graduate school, I wanted to explore more applied areas, taking courses across several areas in engineering and computing. After taking several AI/ML courses, I became fascinated by how mathematical models and somewhat simple computational building blocks could give rise to complex, intelligent behavior. Eventually, I became a teaching assistant for "Machine Learning" and later joined the Dynamic Adaptive Robotic Technologies (DART) Lab. 

My interests center around deep learning, computer vision, multimodal AI, and autonomous systems. I enjoy working with learning-based systems, particularly generative models and perception. I also like understanding how models work from first principles and implementing them myself. Ultimately, I hope to apply my background in AI to autonomous systems and other problems with clear real-world applications.

Outside of technical work, I enjoy hobbies such as yoga, running, swimming, going on walks, piano, art, chess, and brazilian jiu-jitsu.
